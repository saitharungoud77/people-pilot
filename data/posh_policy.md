## Why this policy exists

AIT Solutions wants every person who works here - employees, interns,
contractors and visitors - to feel safe at work. Sexual harassment is
not tolerated in any form, by anyone, whatever their designation.

This policy explains what counts as sexual harassment, how to raise a
complaint, what happens after a complaint is made, and what AIT
Solutions will do about it.

## Who it applies to

This policy applies across every AIT Solutions workplace, including our
office in Hi-Tech City, and to:

- All employees, at every level, whether permanent or on contract.
- Interns, trainees, consultants and contractors.
- Visitors, clients, vendors and anyone else attending the workplace.

It covers incidents at the office, at company events, on company travel,
and in any work-related conversation or online interaction.

## What sexual harassment means

Sexual harassment is any unwelcome act or behaviour of a sexual nature,
directly or by implication, whether or not the person concerned agreed
to it. It includes:

- Unwelcome physical contact or advances.
- A demand or request for sexual favours.
- Sexually coloured remarks or comments about someone's body or dress.
- Showing or circulating pornography.
- Any other unwelcome verbal, non-verbal or physical conduct of a
  sexual nature.

It also covers conduct where a condition of employment or work is
directly or indirectly made to depend on someone's acceptance of a
sexual advance, or where accepting an advance is treated as a reason for
preferential treatment. Harassment may be a single serious incident or
a repeated pattern that makes the workplace hostile, humiliating or
intimidating.

## What is not acceptable

In practice, the following are treated as sexual harassment:

- Sending or forwarding offensive jokes, memes, images or videos,
  including on company chat groups.
- Persistent and unwelcome attention, personal comments or invitations
  after someone has said no.
- Staring, leering, inappropriate gestures, or standing too close.
- Following someone to their home, vehicle or accommodation.
- Threats made in the course of, or connected to, any sexual
  interaction.
- Retaliation against someone for reporting an incident or supporting a
  person who reported.

## Internal Committee (ICC)

AIT Solutions has constituted an Internal Committee as required by the
Act. The committee is required to have these members:

| Position | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Presiding Officer | A senior woman employee of AIT Solutions |
| Employee members | Not fewer than two employees, with at least half the committee being women |
| External member | A person from outside the company, familiar with issues of sexual harassment (from an NGO or association working for women) |

Current member names are notified to every employee by email and on the
notice board. To ask who currently sits on the committee, write to
**posh@aitsolutions.com** or contact HR.

The committee keeps a register of all complaints. Details of any
complaint are entered in it, and the register is not shared outside the
committee.

## How to raise a complaint

1. Put your complaint in writing. It should say what happened, when and
   where it happened, who was involved and any witness you know of.
2. Send it to the Internal Committee at **posh@aitsolutions.com**, or
   hand it to the Presiding Officer or to HR in person.
3. Make the complaint within three months of the incident. The
   committee may extend this by another three months if there is a good
   reason for the delay.

If you find it difficult to write the complaint yourself, tell any
member of the committee. They will write it down as you describe it,
read it back to you and take your signature before proceeding.

You may complain about someone who is senior to you, a client or a
visitor. Seniority makes no difference.

## What happens after a complaint

- The committee acknowledges the complaint and starts an inquiry within
  seven days of receiving it.
- Both the complainant and the person complained against are given a
  chance to be heard, and may bring someone along for support.
- The inquiry is conducted following the principles of natural justice,
  and is completed within ninety days. Written reasons are recorded for
  the conclusion reached.
- If the committee is satisfied that the complainant may face harm, it
  may recommend interim measures within forty-eight hours - for example
  transferring one of the parties, or granting the complainant leave -
  while the inquiry runs.
- The findings and the action taken are shared with both parties in
  writing.

## Confidentiality

The complainant, the respondent and every member of the committee must
keep the complaint and the inquiry confidential. Information may be
shared only where it is needed to carry out the inquiry or to meet a
legal requirement. Disclosing complaint details outside this will itself
be treated as misconduct.

## Protection from retaliation

No one will be punished, threatened, penalised or disadvantaged for
raising a complaint, for supporting someone who raised one, or for
testifying as a witness. Any attempt to take revenge on a complainant is
a serious breach of this policy and will be dealt with under the
company's disciplinary rules.

A complaint that is not proved does not by itself mean the complainant
has done anything wrong.

## False or vexatious complaints

If an inquiry finds that a complaint was deliberately false, frivolous or
vexatious, the committee may recommend action against the person who
made it. A complaint that is simply not proved is not treated as false.

## Employer duties

AIT Solutions will:

- Display a copy of the Act and of this policy at the office and make it
  available on this portal.
- Include sexual harassment as misconduct in the service rules, with the
  penalty for it written out.
- Organise regular awareness sessions so every employee knows what the
  policy covers and how to complain.
- Give every member of the committee the training they need to run an
  inquiry.

## Appeals

Either party may appeal the conclusion of an inquiry to a court or
industrial tribunal within ninety days of being told the outcome.

## Questions

Write to **posh@aitsolutions.com**, or ask HR. If you are unsure whether
something counts as harassment, ask before it gets worse - there is no
penalty for raising a concern that turns out to be minor.
